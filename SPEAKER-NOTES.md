## Script

### 1. Intro · John · ~0:20

Hi everyone. We’re Joshua, Callen, John and Wiley, and our project is building the on-ramp: getting AI tools and entrepreneurship to students who don’t have a way in yet.

That photo behind me is from one of our workshops this month.

### 2. Meet NGF · John · ~0:40

So who are we working with? Next Generation Founders, or NGF: a student-run nonprofit John co-founded about a year ago with Rishi Sadanandan.

NGF works in three layers. Ignite: free, hands-on AI and entrepreneurship workshops. Access: tool credits and resources so students can keep building after the workshop. And Community: peers, mentors and follow-up support beyond a single session.

It’s free for students and schools, and no prior experience is required.

### 3. Team · John · ~0:45

Quickly, who we are: four Stanford students. John is a senior; Callen, Wiley and Josh are juniors.

John studies CS on the AI track, has worked at Lockheed Martin, and co-founded NGF, so he’s been leading these workshops from day one. Josh studies CS and Math and is Chief of Staff for APAC at Cognition, the AI company behind Devin, so he lives inside the AI tools we’re teaching. Callen studies math and does digital media for Stanford Athletics, which is our outreach and storytelling. Wiley studies Mathematical and Computational Science, which fits the measurement side of our 30-day test.

Why us? We use these AI tools every day, we’re close in age to the students we want to reach, and we can show up in person on their campuses.

### 4. Story · Callen · ~0:45

Meet Maya. She’s a composite, not one person, but you’ve met her. She’s at a community college and she has an idea: study groups for students who work nights, like she does.

(click) She has a laptop. (click) But the AI tools that could turn her idea into a product in an afternoon cost about twenty dollars a month, each. (click) And nobody she knows has ever started a company.

(click) At Stanford, both of those come almost by default. Maya gets neither. So we brought both to her campus. Watch her screen.

### 5. The gap · Callen · ~0:50

Is there actually a need? This is from the Digital Education Council’s 2026 global survey, over 45,000 responses. 88 percent of students already use AI in their studies. Every dot is one percent.

(click) But in the US and Canada, only 17 percent of students think their instructors are well equipped to guide them on it. Almost everyone is using it; almost nobody is being shown how. And community college students, with the least time and money, are the ones most likely to fall through that gap.

(click) Then there’s cost. ChatGPT, Cursor, Lovable: the good versions are subscriptions. For students choosing between a textbook and a tank of gas, that’s a wall.

### 6. The brief · Callen · ~0:30

Here’s where we started. NGF sits between two groups: club leaders at campuses NGF has never worked with, and AI companies like OpenAI, Cursor and Lovable who want their tools in students’ hands.

(click) Our class project strengthens both sides. On the campus side: find new campuses and club leaders, reach out, and explain what NGF does. On the AI side: map and qualify new partners and build a repeatable ask. Once we got into it, we realized the brief could go further.

### 7. Mission · John · ~0:40

This is NGF’s north star, in John’s words: students should be creators, not just consumers, of AI.

AI has made the barrier to building lower than it has ever been. The risk is that only some students get to use that. So the mission is to make entrepreneurship and AI accessible to every student, regardless of background or technical experience.

Our vision: every campus has an on-ramp. Any student can walk into a workshop and walk out having built something. That’s the systemic change: not one event, but a door that stays open.

### 8. Statements · John · ~1:00

NGF already has strong statements on its website. We kept their spirit and tightened each one so it does one job, the way established organizations write them.

(click) Mission is what we do, for whom, and the result: to give under-resourced students the skills, tools, and community to build with AI and turn their ideas into impact. Skills, tools and community map directly to NGF’s three layers: Ignite, Access, Community.

(click) Purpose is why we exist, in one line: so that a student’s background never decides whether they get to build.

(click) Vision is the world we’re working toward: a future where every campus is an on-ramp, and every young person can build with AI to change their community. That’s the systemic change: not one great workshop, but a permanent door on every campus, renewed each year through student clubs.

### 9. Traction · John · ~0:30

NGF already has early traction: more than 300 students reached, more than 20 colleges, and over five thousand dollars in AI credits distributed. Lovable is already a technology partner.

(The 300 figure is the team’s own count this month; the website still says 200+.) So NGF can get students into the room. Our project is about what comes around that.

### 10. Our take · Wiley · ~1:10

This is the heart of what our team added. We kept the brief and widened it four ways. (Hover a row to flash its photo.)

(click) Not just community colleges: any campus where students lack access. That’s how our first workshop ended up at Menlo College, a small private college.

(click) Not just startup clubs: any club whose members want to build. Menlo’s host was the International Business Club. That multiplies the doors on every campus.

(click) Not just introductions: a hands-on build. Students watch an idea become a working website, live.

(click) And not one-off events: a channel that renews. Clubs plan programming every year, and Foothill invited NGF back for a second year in a row.

### 11. How we found them · Wiley · ~1:00

How we actually found and connected with each stop.

Menlo: we deliberately looked past startup clubs and community colleges, found the International Business Club, and connected with Crystal Romero, who hosted NGF’s first workshop of 2026. (Add one line on how the first message went out.)

Foothill: NGF ran a workshop there last year, so we went back through Weng Han Fong, who leads the Startup Club. He invited us back for year two. That’s the renewable channel.

And the AI side: we mapped the tools students actually use. Lovable is already a partner and its credits go out at our workshops. Replit, Canva and StreetCode are prospects we’re reaching out to now, with a specific ask for each.

### 12. The build · Wiley · ~0:40

This is the moment that lands in the room. We type an idea, in plain English, into Lovable. Here it’s Maya’s idea: study groups for students who work nights.

Within minutes there’s a working website. Not a mockup. Something you could share that night. AI has made the barrier to building lower than ever, and we want every student to see that with their own eyes. (Click the browser to replay.)

### 13. Menlo · Josh · ~0:40

Stop one: Menlo College, on a Tuesday. A brand-new relationship, and NGF’s first workshop of 2026. Our host was Crystal Romero and the International Business Club. Not a startup club, and that’s exactly the point.

We covered startup fundamentals, walked through how funding rounds work, and showed how Lovable turns an idea into a working website. Look at that room.

### 14. Foothill · Wiley · ~0:40

Stop two, the following Wednesday: Foothill College. Weng Han Fong, who leads the Startup Club, invited NGF back for the second year in a row. That’s the renewable channel working.

Same fundamentals and live build, and this crowd wanted to go deeper: the Bay Area startup scene, how founders handle competition, famous pivots, and how successful companies think about debt versus equity.

### 15. What next · Josh · ~0:40

NGF can get students into the room, and they leave having built something. The unanswered question is what happens next.

We think about it as four steps: attend, build, return, share. (click) Our hypothesis is that the big drop-off happens after the workshop, between building and coming back. To be clear, that’s a question we want to test, not a number NGF has measured yet. The fix we want to test is simple: a check-in on day one and day seven, like these, with a clear next step.

### 16. Partners · Josh · ~0:45

On the AI side, Lovable is already a partner. We’re investigating three more. None of these are committed; they’re prospects.

Replit, for AI coding: the ask is education credits or demo sessions, so students who want to keep building software can. Canva, for design and AI creativity: tools, workshops or design mentors, to make prototypes and storytelling approachable. And StreetCode, for community tech education: co-hosting or a curriculum exchange, to reach learners through channels they already trust.

A partnership has to work for everyone: the partner gives tools and expertise, NGF gives hands-on delivery and student access, schools give a trusted network and a room, and students gain confidence and real projects.

### 17. 30-day test · Josh · ~0:45

Here’s the test our team can own over the next 30 days. Week one, discover: build a shortlist, contact potential partners, capture what they need. Weeks two and three, design: develop one joint workshop or follow-up pilot with a willing partner. Week four, measure: collect baseline feedback and check in with participants after seven days.

We’ll track partner replies, attendance, first projects and seven-day continuation. Success means evidence of continued learning, not just headcount.

Risks we’re watching: one-off enthusiasm, so we add a seven-day follow-up and a simple next step; dependence on one sponsor, so we diversify partners; and unclear impact, so we run a quick pulse before and after plus a later check-in.

### 18. Stakeholders · Callen · ~0:45

Four stakeholder groups. At the center are students: they get free entry, tools, confidence and a first project.

Around them: schools and clubs, who give trusted campus access and a room; AI organizations, who bring credits and volunteers and get a new generation of builders; and mentors and volunteers, who give practical coaching and follow-through.

(click) Students do have alternatives: one-off talks, paid courses, YouTube. But the biggest competitor is time. These students are busy.

(click) Our edge: we’re peers close to their age, we show up in person on their campus, and they leave having built something, not just having watched.

### 19. Our impact · Callen · ~0:40

So what exists now that wouldn’t without our team? A pipeline: a researched list of campuses and club leaders NGF had never contacted. More doors: looking past community colleges and startup clubs is literally what got us into Menlo. A partner playbook: researched targets and a repeatable ask NGF can reuse. And a follow-up test, so NGF can measure who keeps building, not just who showed up.

The deliverable is a system NGF can keep using after our quarter ends.

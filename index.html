So who are we working with? Next Generation Founders, or NGF: a student-run nonprofit John co-founded about a year ago with Rishi Sadanandan.

NGF works in three layers. Ignite: free, hands-on AI and entrepreneurship workshops. Access: tool credits and resources so students can keep building after the workshop. And Community: peers, mentors and follow-up support beyond a single session.

It’s free for students and schools, and no prior experience is required.

Creators,not justconsumers.

AI and entrepreneurship for every student, any background.

This is NGF’s north star, in John’s words: students should be creators, not just consumers, of AI.

AI has made the barrier to building lower than it has ever been. The risk is that only some students get to use that. So the mission is to make entrepreneurship and AI accessible to every student, regardless of background or technical experience.

Our vision: every campus has an on-ramp. Any student can walk into a workshop and walk out having built something. That’s the systemic change: not one event, but a door that stays open.

NGF can get students into the room, and they leave having built something. The unanswered question is what happens next.

We think about it as four steps: attend, build, return, share. (click) Our hypothesis is that the big drop-off happens after the workshop, between building and coming back. To be clear, that’s a question we want to test, not a number NGF has measured yet. The fix we want to test is simple: a check-in on day one and day seven, like these, with a clear next step.

On the AI side, Lovable is already a partner. We’re investigating three more. None of these are committed; they’re prospects.

Replit, for AI coding: the ask is education credits or demo sessions, so students who want to keep building software can. Canva, for design and AI creativity: tools, workshops or design mentors, to make prototypes and storytelling approachable. And StreetCode, for community tech education: co-hosting or a curriculum exchange, to reach learners through channels they already trust.

A partnership has to work for everyone: the partner gives tools and expertise, NGF gives hands-on delivery and student access, schools give a trusted network and a room, and students gain confidence and real projects.

Here’s the test our team can own over the next 30 days. Week one, discover: build a shortlist, contact potential partners, capture what they need. Weeks two and three, design: develop one joint workshop or follow-up pilot with a willing partner. Week four, measure: collect baseline feedback and check in with participants after seven days.

We’ll track partner replies, attendance, first projects and seven-day continuation. Success means evidence of continued learning, not just headcount.

Risks we’re watching: one-off enthusiasm, so we add a seven-day follow-up and a simple next step; dependence on one sponsor, so we diversify partners; and unclear impact, so we run a quick pulse before and after plus a later check-in.
